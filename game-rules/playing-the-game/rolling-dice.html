Rolling dice

Whenever an action asks for a roll, you go through the following steps:

  1. Determine the roll difficulty. This will always be a number between 1 and 12. It’s determined by the GM, or written as part of the ability description. A single roll can have multiple difficulties.

Example

You are rolling Diff 3 (Body) to climb up a tree. The difficulty of the roll is 3.

  1. Determine if you are skilled in the action. If an action benefits from your skill, you add your Skill die to the roll.

Example

Your character is skilled at climbing, you get to add your a d6 (your Skill die) to your roll.

  1. Assemble the dice you will roll. Expend dice from your Might, Body, Soul, and Drive die pools, and add them to this roll. You can expend at most 15 dice. A single roll can use at most 16 dice; 15 from the pool and 1 Skill Die.

Example

You expend 2d6 from your Might pool and 1d6 from your Body pool. Your entire roll will therefore be 4d6, including the Skill die.

  1. Roll the dice. Roll all the dice, and apply any roll modifiers. Even if the role has multiple difficulties or multiple targets, you only make one roll. You roll 4d6, and get: 2, 3, 1, 6.
  2. Determine if roll succeeded. When any of the dice rolls on a number equal to or above the difficulty, it counts as a success. If you have at least one successful roll, the entire roll is success. In rolls with multiple difficulties, you have do this for every difficulty.

Example

The difficulty of the roll was 3, therefore you have 2 successes (rolls 3 and 6). Since you have at least 1 success, the entire roll is successful.

  1. Determine the Amp. Every success die above the first is considered an amp(lification) die, improving your outcome. Count all of the extra success you have, and check the description of your Action and Abilities you used to see if any of them have an Amp option.

Example

Your roll had 2 successes (rolls 3 and 6). That means you had 1 extra success above what you needed. This means you have 1 Amp.
You check your abilities, and find out you have an ability, that allows you to become familiar with anything you climb enough to automatically take someone up with you. The ability allows you to do guide number of people equal to your Amp.

  1. Resolve the consequences. Tell the results of your roll, the Amp and its effects, to your GM. Together, you narrate the consequences of the roll.

Example

You manage to climb up the tree with ease. Thanks to your Amp, you grab one of your party members to go up with you. You both climb up all the way to the top of the tree, surveying the area.

Determining Difficulty

When choosing how difficult a task can be, you have to take into consideration that not all situations and characters are created equal. When hitting an opponent with an attack, setting a single difficulty for all players is a fairly simple concept. On the other hand, how to set a single difficulty in studying an advanced alchemical tome?

In other systems, this question might be answered by using single difficulty and roll modifiers. In our case, we differentiate between objective and subjective difficulty.

Rough Guidelines

Difficulty 5. This difficulty is used for almost all Self-Amp rolls. It should relate to tasks where a regular person must apply their skills and experience to succeed.
Difficulty 6. This is the highest challenge a regular person might face. Backbreaking physical labor, and taxing mental tasks.
Difficulty 8. This difficulty should be one that requires an extremely talent person to succeed.
Difficulty 10. We are reaching difficulties face by very few people in history.
Difficulty 12. This difficulty would be reaching the limits of what is possible. In epic fantasy, these would be on par with Herculean tasks.

Objective difficulty

These almost always come into play when it comes to purely mechanical situations: attacks, defense, Self-amp, scene mechanics. These are usually the core mechanics to your play style, where you desire consistency and some degree of fairness. As vague as this description might be, you will be fine with the following rule of thumb.

Rule of thumb

Everything starts as objective difficulty, and might change into a subjective one after conversation between Player and the GM.

Subjective difficulty

Subjective difficulty is best defined by an example. The player sees group of villagers trying to lift up a stuck wagon. They decide they want to go over, and lift it up, and push it away. The first decision of the GM is to declare it a Diff 6 roll. The player then brings up an ability of theirs. It makes them far stronger then a regular person, allowing them to casually lift 1 ton, and to lift up to 2 tons. Taking this into account, the GM changes it to Diff 2, only to give some small chance to struggle.

Due to this improvised nature, there are no clear rules as to how to decide on a subjective ruling. Instead, best we can do is few guidelines:

  • If the characters natural predisposition should make a task easier, lower the difficulty.
  • If it makes the task trivial, make it automatic success or Diff 2. The Diff 2 gives a small chance of failure, that allows for a bit of variability.
  • When a character lacks predisposition to do something, consider making it an automatic failure. Understanding an advanced alchemical manual when a character barely knows what alchemy is impossible. Even a Diff 12 roll doesn’t change that.
  • When a character is bad at something as character feature, increase the difficulty. Some examples are physically weak, finds books boring, can’t see well.
  • When a character is temporarily held back or at an advantage, consider making the roll look at Using Hindered & Eased.

Using Hindered & Eased

While difficulty is about figuring out how hard it is to achieve the task on its own, the Hindered and Eased mechanics are about allowing temporary advantages to affect the roll. If a character is trying to catch up to someone by sprinting in the mud, their roll with be Hindered. Similarly, trying to outrun someone after leading them into a mud pit will make it Eased.

Rule of Thumb

A single thing that helps or hold back should Ease or Hinder by 1. More severe one by 2. Extreme effect is 3.
You should avoid using larger numbers. These should be reserved for extreme, once in a game rare circumstances.

Stacking Bonuses

Make use of stacking multiple bonuses. Each effect might ease or hinder by 1, eventually stacking up to an overall larger bonus. This will happen quite often in the game, due to player abilities.

Stacking bonuses in combat

If a enemy is knocked prone and has sand thrown into their eyes, each of these would ease a player roll by 1, for total eased by 2 roll.

Additional rules

One action, one roll

Regardless of the number of targets or multiple difficulties, you only ever perform one roll. As such, an action only ever needs you to find one thing you are skilled at that applies, and to only ever expend dice from your pools once. Ability or a Boon also applies to the entire roll.

All dice can succeed

Whenever you make a roll, you may only roll the dice that can succeed. A difficulty 7 roll can only use d8 or larger. You can not include the d6 in the roll.

This become slightly more complex when it comes to multiple difficulties. In such cases the dice have to be able to succeed on at least one of the difficulties.

Example

You are rolling with Diff 6 and Diff 7. Your skill die is d6, and all your pools have only d6 dice. You can still continue with the roll, but you can only succeed on the Diff 6. The Diff 7 automatically fails.

Automatic failure

If you do not have any dice that can succeed on the difficulty of the roll, you automatically fail.

Example

You must succeed on a Diff 10 roll, but your skill die is d8, and the largest die in any of your pools is also d8. You are unable to succeed on this roll and automatically fail.

Example

You must succeed on a Diff 8 roll. You are not skilled in this Action, and therefore can not apply your skill die of d8. You expended all your d8 dice from Pools, and are only left with d6. You have no viable dice, and automatically fail on the roll.

Multiple difficulties

When a roll comes with multiple difficulties, you determine the success of each separately. You roll the dice once, and then count success for each Difficulty.

Example

You roll 4d6, Diff 6 to avoid being hit, and Diff 3 to be disarmed by the attack. You rolled 1, 4, 5, 5. You fail at the Diff 6, but you succeed on the Diff 3 (with 3 successes: 4, 5, 5).

In a scenario when your roll is Eased or Hindered it is important to consider the context of the roll. The roll modifiers should apply to only the dice that can succeed on the roll.

Eased roll with multiple difficulties

You perform an action to cleave 2 enemies with your Axe. Only one of them is heavily armored and trained. Your friend decides to help you out with the stronger one, and uses an ability that Eases attacking them by 1.
Hitting the first one is Diff 5, while hitting the other is Diff 7 Eased by 1. You dice to go all in, and rolling 3d6+4d8. You roll (2, 3, 2) on the d6, and (5, 6, 2, 4) on the d8.
You can re-roll one failed die because the attack is eased. Since it was the Diff 7 attack that was Eased, you can only choose one of the d8 dice to re-roll.

Eased roll with multiple difficulties, cont.

Lets change the previous example. This time, The Diff 5 roll is Eased by 1. Since all of the dice you used can succeed on the Diff 5, you can re-roll any d6 or d8 dice. You choose d8 and roll 7, succeeding on both attacks.

Hindered roll with multiple difficulties

This time, your attack against armored enemy is hindered, and you manage to roll (2, 3, 2) on d6, and (8, 6, 2, 4) on d8. You must re-roll the one successful die. Since the the Diff 7 is the one Hindered, you must re-roll the only successful d8 roll. You end up rolling 5, and the both attacks fail.

Roll modifiers

The game uses two special modifiers: Eased and Hindered. Both of these allow you to re-roll dice during your roll. They share these rules:

  • Single die can only be re-rolled once.
  • If Eased or Hindered, you must re-roll if possible.
  • Eased and Hindered cancel each other out. If a roll is Eased by 2 and Hindered by 1, the roll will end up being Eased by 1.
  • If your roll is Eased by number larger then number of dice you rolled, miss out on extra re-roll. You can not re-roll same die twice.
  • If your roll is Hindered by number greater then number of dice you rolled, you avoid the extra re-rolls. You can not re-roll the same die twice.
  • You choose which of the dice gets re-rolled, as long as it is a die that can succeed on that difficulty.

Eased

Re-roll one of the failed dice in your roll.

Example

You are rolling Diff 6 Eased by 1. You rolled 3d6 for result of (2, 5, 3). You choose to re-roll the first die, rolling 6 and succeeding on the roll.

Hindered

Re-roll one of the success dice in your roll.

Example

You are rolling Diff 6 Hindered by 1. You rolled 3d8 for result of (2, 6, 7). You choose the second die, rolling 8 and succeeding on the roll with Amp 1s.

Multiple targets

If an action has more then one target, your roll will have a difficulty for each of the targets. The success will be resolved as a regular roll with multiple difficulties.
