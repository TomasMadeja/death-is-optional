Skills

Any time your character is supposed to be exceptionally good at something, they gain a skill in this thing. This mechanic is similar to proficiency in D&D and Pathfinder, skills in Cypher System, or skills in Draw Steel.

For the most part, there is no specific list of skills in this system. Instead, they are formulated with a single word, phrase, or sentence. For guidance on how to come up with a Skill, see Creating Skills.

Skills

  • Climbing
  • Navigating Forests
  • Using Swords
  • Driving Vehicles
  • Carpentry
  • Creative writing
  • Learning & remembering surface level information

When taking an action, you should consult your list of skills. If any of the skills applies to the given action, you may use your skill die to the roll. Skill die does not count towards the 15 dice limit of a roll. You only add it to your roll once.

Skill Die

Your skill die starts as d4 at level 1, and increases by size every odd numbered character level:

  • d4 at level 1
  • d6 at level 3
  • d8 at level 5
  • d10 at level 7
  • d12 at level 9

Abilities & Skills

Your character is skilled at using an ability if it was gained from a perk.

What if two skills apply to single roll?

You only add the skill die once, regardless of how many skills might apply to the action.

Do i add skill die if it can not succeed on the roll?

No.
Even if you rolled, the Skill die can’t succeed on the roll because it is too small.

Impossible difficulty

Your skill die is d6, but the roll is Diff 7. Unfortunately, you won’t be able to use the skill die, even when you are skilled at this action. You can still expend dice from your pool and attempt a roll.

One of the multiple difficulties is impossible

The roll has two difficulties: Diff 5 and Diff 7. Your skill die is d6. You may roll the skill die. When resolving the action, your skill die only counts towards the Diff 5.

Does skill die count towards ability cost?

No. Ability cost must be paid using dice from pools. Skill die is only used to give you an edge.

Creating Skills

We’ll split up creation process of an in-game skill into these steps:

  1. Come up with a general description of the skill. This step is meant for both player and GM to have some idea. The two questions you want to answer is: What kind of actions does it apply to? How do you want to use it?
  2. Adjust the scope based on your game.
  3. Shorten the skill name.

Figuring out the scope

Not all skills are created equal. Imagine playing a game themed around typical dungeon fantasy. Character skilled in Using a sword, Fighting undead or Disabling traps gets to apply their skill generously. Meanwhile, a character with skill in Philosophy or Comparative Sociology will rarely get an option to do so. This is the scope of the skill.

When creating a skill, you must take into account the type of game the character is used in. At the end of the day, this process will boil down to answering the question “How often do i get to use the skill for different actions?”:

  • Is it used for almost every (different) action? Make the scope of the skill smaller.
  • Do i rarely, or never, get to use the skill? Increase the scope of the skill.

Is the scope always the reason why skill is overused/under-used?

Sometimes, skill doesn’t come into play for gameplay reasons. Skill in Disabling traps is a well-worded one, but might end up unused for several sessions if the dungeon has no traps. Skills don’t have to be used constantly and can always be revisited in the future. If it turns out that the skill becomes useless to a character, it’s time to revisit the skill.

Changing skill

Changing the formulation of skill should always be combined effort of both the player and the GM.

On the other hand, some skills might be used too often because they are Core Skills to the game.

Formulating the scope of skills isn’t an exact science. Some skills will be central to the way the game is played, and could be considered Core Skills. For the rest, you can use the following advice:

  • Does the game revolve around using this skill? If so, you should consider making it smaller. Being skilled in Biology will have a different impact in a dungeon fantasy, then in a sci-fi fantasy where players investigate an abandoned research lab.
  • If the scope is too small, you can bundle multiple related skills together. Skill in Disabling traps might become Studying & manipulating mechanisms (locks, traps, etc.). Skill in recognizing edible mushrooms could become Foraging, poisonous flora.
  • If the scope is too small for areas of science or crafting, try searching for fields that encompass the one you want to be skilled it. Skill in Microbiology could become Biology.
  • When a skill’s scope is too large, look into specific fields with science or craftsmanship. If that won’t help, use the specific activity.

Core Skills

These skills usually come into play often and are integral to the gameplay. However, if you were to reduce their scope too much, the players will be forced into having only these Core Skills. In most games, these will be related to combat, persuasion, or lying.

When choosing skills, gaining Core Skills is usually more restricted and more desired by players. The general rule is, that if a player has an option to choose any Skill, they are not allowed to pick a Core Skill for your game.

Epic Fantasy: Offense

In epic fantasy, you might choose between groups of weapons, types of weapons, or fighting styles.

Fighting styles

  • Swordsmanship
  • Fencing
  • Dual wielding
  • Archery

Weapons

  • Maces & Clubs
  • Spears
  • Bows
  • Axes

Weapon types

  • Slashing weapons
  • Light weapons
  • Heavy Weapons
  • Oversized Weapons
  • Ranged weapons
    You might also choose allow the combinations of each of these. This would be the recommended approach, as gives the players an option to express themselves.

Epic Fantasy: Defense

While fighting, the character must be able to defend themselves. You have variety of options on how to implement this:

Type of damage

  • Elemental
  • Mental
  • Physical
  • Decaying

Type of attack / Source of damage

  • Magical
  • Weapon
  • Physical
  • Poisons
  • Disease
  • Natural

Fighting styles

  • Swordsmanship
  • Fencing
  • Dual wielding
  • Archery
  • Magecraft

Once again, combination of these is the best solution. This way, a player can combine their play-style into a Core Skill, avoiding inflation of overly-granular skills. Character skilled in Fencing will be skilled at both attack and defense with their rapier, and still retain a weakness to defending against mental attacks.

Attack & Defense in other formats

In games relying more on social scenes, the attack & defense can appear in other forms. While in a interacting a suspect, both parties are engaging in a form of verbal attack and defense. During a meeting of two factions, both sides are locked in a combat to try to convince, misdirect, lie to and see through the other side.

Social "offensive" skills

  • Lying about self
  • Storytelling
  • Creating detailed stories
  • Attention to detail
  • Truthfulness
  • Empathy
  • Mirroring & Matching
  • Argumentative
  • Aggressiveness
  • Blackmailing
  • Faking Emotions

Social "defensive" skills

  • Stubbornness
  • Verbal Analysis
  • Empathy
  • Attention to detail
  • Reading emotions
  • Observant
  • Following complicated threads of information
  • Surface level knowledge of topics
  • Easily remembering information
