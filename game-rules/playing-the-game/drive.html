Drive

Your drive consists of Drive pool, Drive Emotion and Drives.

Drive pool

Unlike Might, Body, or Soul pools, drive pool isn’t a simple collection of dice. It is instead separated into groups of dice called drive levels. Each level is a group of 4 dice. All dice in the drive level start out as d6, and change in size as you level up.

The drive pool start out empty, and restoring dice follows these rules:

  • Dice are restored starting from lowest to highest drive level.
  • Within the drive level, you restore dice from the lowest to highest size.
  • Any time you restore dice in the drive pool, you roll d4. You regain the number of dice rolled.
  • You restore dice in the pool, when an ability, action or scene relates to one of your Drives, and causes your character to feel the Drive Emotion.

Drive Emotion

Every player character, and some NPCs, have a specific drive emotion. The drive emotion is a narrative tool and describes what feeling fuels your actions. It also influences your interactions with others. When creating a character, you choose one of the emotions written down on the lines in the diagram below, referred to as complex emotions or drive emption. The emotions written down in the circles are referred to as base emotions.

Interactions between drives

When 2 characters (or character & NPC with drive) interact, their drives might help or hinder them. Every drive emotion is made of 2 base emotions. You can find out what these emotions are by looking at the diagram, finding your drive emotion, and looking at what base emotions the line connects to. Based on this, any two characters fall into these two categories:

  • Compatible. The characters share one base emotion. When working together, the rolls are eased by 1.

  • Incompatible. The characters share no base emotion. When working together, the rolls are hindered by 1.

  • Rivals. The characters have identical drive emotion. When working together, the rolls are hindered by 2. When facing each other as opponents, restore drive pool every round.

What would be considered working together?

Any ability intended to assist the other character. As a counterexample, attacking the same opponent is not considered working together. Working together requires helping the other character in the same way. This would also include a seemingly unrelated ability that will ease the other person’s roll.

Can drive emotion change?

Yes. Drive emotion can and should change through out the game. The player can decide to change it, and is forced to change it as part of their character arcs. Changing the drive emotion signifies character development.

Drives

Drives are sentences, that describe what triggers your drive. They describe something important to you, be it memory, lesson, rule, or an emotional bond. When something that relates to this drive happens during the scene, it causes you to restore dice in your Drive pool.

Creating a drive.

Overall, drive can be any sentence. There are no strict rules as to how a drive should look, only advice on how to write one.

Style 1: Bonds

This style takes inspiration from the Memento Mori (by 2LM). You formulate drive as Subject -Phrase. For example: Sheriff Giles - Crime does not pay.

The subject is something or someone this drive is tied to. It can be a place, person, memory, book, idea, or anything else you can think of. The subject might be the source of your drive, the person who caused or instilled this idea in you. It can be it’s object, something you desire. Or it can be merely something that thematically fits, such as a sword symbolizing strength.

The phrase is a sentence that carries the spirit of the drive. It can be an expression, quote, or any phrase that fits the spirit of your drive. It provides a brief summary of the idea behind it. It should be specific enough, so that it matches your idea. The reason why it’s limited to single sentence is, so as not to tie it down in details. Your drive should remain flexible enough, so that you can apply it to multitudes of situations.

In addition, you may write down a longer description of the drive in your notes, in case you want to better define it.

How does a drive relate to drive emotion?

These two are inadvertently linked when trying to apply them during the game. Drive on it’s own can be fairly vague, and cover loads of situations. The drive emotion narrows the drive down, to also feed into the emotions. In other words, it’s not enough that the situation is related to your drive, it must make your character feel the given emotion.

We’ll use an example of Father - Revenge upon the oppressors! as a drive. One instilled upon our character by our father, teaching us to fight oppression. The most straightforward drive emotion would be anger. In such combination, the drive might be triggered upon the sight of people being oppressed, making us enraged.

However, what if we choose fear as our drive emotion? We must now think of “How does this situation make us feel afraid?”. In this case, our character might be afraid to act and fight the oppressors, triggering our drive.

When does a drive trigger dice restoration?

Your drive can be triggered by either the description (window dressing), scene itself, or by an ability (actions of others). We will refer to both of these as trigger.

Drive can be triggered if the answer to all of these questions is yes:

  • Is the trigger narrative? (Is the trigger not about game mechanics.)
  • Is the trigger relevant to your drive?
  • Can you describe how the trigger makes your character feel? Does the description match your drive?
  • Does the trigger make your character feel your drive emotion?

Some simple rules to keep in mind:

  • Drive can’t be triggered by game mechanics. For example: Your character got 1 major wound.
  • Simple bread and butter things like fighting multiple enemies, taking large amount of wounds, or having low pool, are treated as mechanical. Situations like fear of death or fighting overwhelming odds must be the narrative stakes of the scene.
  • Specific drive can only be triggered once per round.

Sparks of Inspiration

Spark of inspiration is a special die, gained by restoring all dice in the drive level. Once you do so, and even after you expend some of them, you have an access to a single d20 dice called the spark of inspiration. You gain 1 such die for each drive level, up to 5 total.

You may use the die when rolling, expending it. It does not count towards the 15 die limit.
Choose one of these effects for the spark of inspiration, that only occur if the die itself is successful:

  • Simple power-up: There is no special effect, beyond the powerup from extra die.
  • Expand scope: Expand the scope of what your ability can target or do. If an ability allows you to affect metals, you may affect a mineral. An ability that produces heat might instead cool things down.
  • Fine Control: You can control the area of effect of your ability to the point of avoiding specific targets, creating precise shape, regardless of the ability. For example, you can create an explosion that avoids specific people.
  • Overpower: Do not count the spark of inspiration towards the Amp and Self-Amp. Instead, if it counts as a success, it applies the full Amp and Self-Amp regardless of the conditions such as “Every 3rd”.

Spark of Inspiration: Overpower

You use an ability “Fire Explosion” that deals +1 minor wound in 5 meter radius. It has Amp: Each amp: +1 wound. Each 3rd amp: +1 major wound; and Self-Amp: Each amp: +5 meters affected radius. Every 3rd amp: double the radius.

You choose to roll 2x d6 and 1 spark of inspiration, and end up rolling (5, 6, 19). Your spark does not count towards the Amp and Self-Amp, and therefore they are both 2. Your current damage is 3 minor wounds, and the fireball explodes in 15 meter radius.

The spark dice allows you to apply the full bonus, therefore giving you: +1 minor wound, +1 major wound, +5 radius, double radius.

The full effect of the ability is therefore: 4 minor wounds, 1 major wound, 40 meter radius.

Using the spark of inspiration has the following conditions:

  • You can not use spark of inspiration to succeed on a roll, you would not otherwise be able to succeed on. The roll must include at least one other die that can succeed on the roll difficulty.
  • Spark of inspiration must not be the only die rolled.
  • Only one spark of inspiration can be used in a roll

After using the spark of inspiration, the die is expended, and blocked. While blocked, the spark for this drive level can not be restored. To unblock it, you must expend all dice in your Drive pool.
